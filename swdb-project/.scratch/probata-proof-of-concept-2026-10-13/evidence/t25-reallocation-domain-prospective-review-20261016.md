# Ticket25 prospective qualified ownership/domain repair

Date: 2026-10-16 ET. Source/domain review only, not a proof or executed diagnostic. Original `swdb/std_models.py` SHA-256 `cf708cf2debd8abc525bd97c8fd30c6117ebff98dddd24978f97d34cbf2ad9c2` remains the reference. No repository edit, compiler, verifier, test, SSH or actual job was performed.

The proposed qualified v3 direction is sound after the allocation-identity refinement below. A predicate consisting only of `valid(v)`, freeability and byte separation from the advertised capacity range is insufficient for safe growth. Preserve v2 and its named contract-gap negatives; do not describe v3 proofs as unchanged-v2 proofs.

## Required lifetime boundary

For nonzero capacity, require receiver allocation identity different from the backing allocation identity, alongside original valid/initialized fields, `size <= capacity <= max`, writable capacity extent and initialized size prefix, plus freeable backing base. In ACSL terms, the intended survival fact is `base_addr(v) != base_addr(v->data)` when capacity is positive; in the existing heap translation it is distinct object identities. These are semantic design clauses, not permission to bypass the public vocabulary checker. `acsl_subset.py` currently refuses `base_addr` and other unadmitted memory builtins: a qualified source/method/checker vocabulary extension or another explicit source-level ownership representation must be reviewed before public use.

Full capacity *byte* separation does not imply allocation separation. A single malloc block can contain six advertised ints at offsets0..23 and a suitably aligned24-byte receiver at offset32. Let receiver.data point to the malloc base, size0, capacity6, and grow to7. The receiver and advertised array bytes are disjoint, the pointer is freeable, and the initialized-prefix condition is vacuous. Freeing the base still kills the receiver before its final field writes. A receiver embedded in the unused tail of a larger backing allocation must therefore be excluded. Conversely, requiring `freeable(v)` would unnecessarily exclude ordinary stack receivers. Exact backing extent equal to capacity bytes would be an additional conservative restriction, rather than the minimal distinct-allocation condition.

The relevant normative distinction is between byte-location separation and allocated-block identity; `freeable` identifies a releasable allocation base, and allocation frames distinguish pre-state frees from post-state allocates. See [ACSL1.22, sections2.7.1–2.7.3](https://www.frama-c.com/download/acsl-1.22.pdf). The source counterstates above are independent static reasoning, not solver or sanitizer observations.

`capacity==0` must retain the existing `data==NULL` convention and guard all backing-base/freeability queries. `size==0,capacity>0` still requires allocation separation and freeability. Do not initialize all spare capacity as an ownership requirement: only the size prefix is initialized. The original constructors avoid zero-size allocation and grow requests positive capacity; a non-null zero-byte allocation is a separate unsupported representation case, not automatically UB.

## Freeability is not exclusive ownership

Two distinct receivers can share one freeable backing base and each satisfy a unary freeable/separate predicate. That suffices for some unary function proofs, but does not give two simultaneous deallocation rights or preservation of both vector invariants after one release. Do not infer exclusive ownership from `freeable`.

For the smallest one-vector vertical, retain a unary deallocatable-buffer domain and prove each call's requirements. For multi-vector composition, additionally establish and preserve allocation-owner/resource uniqueness, or explicitly invalidate an aliased vector view after release and refuse its later owning call. Ordinary escaped element/data pointers remain allowed; ownership rights and pointer values are different. After growth, old aliases retain their old identity/offset and become stale; aliases of surviving allocations remain valid. No global no-alias premise or finite identity/epoch bound is justified.

## Frames and allocation clauses require a separate complete audit

Adding an owned predicate alone is not the whole repair. Enumerate pre-existing live objects, old backing allocation, new fresh allocation, receivers, escaped aliases, initialized cells and allocation status at every source step. The grow copy modifies fresh storage, release kills the old block, and final writes modify the surviving receiver. Frame only surviving allocation identities; preserve issuance history and old-pointer invalidity.

Use explicit reallocation/non-reallocation behaviors where they remove ambiguity. Non-growing push writes the old tail cell; a full buffer's old `data[size]` is one-past and must not be treated as an admitted pre-state writable append cell. Reallocating push/resize write a new allocation, including a copied prefix, not an old-buffer cell recovered by numerical equality. Check the selected verifier's assigns/allocation semantics rather than silently broadening or dropping a frame obligation. `allocates`/`frees` permit changes and do not by themselves prove allocation, release, non-null success, freshness or no-growth stability. Preserve every original postcondition in the new model and separately prove strengthened owned postconditions. Existing helper `assigns *this`, wrapper buffer frames, conditional frees/allocates, initialized-prefix loops and source/destination cross effects all need checked VCs, not just structural acceptance.

## Smallest complete qualified public vertical

Start with one int vector: qualified v3 count/value constructor, qualified `reserve`, its actual grow helper and qualified destructor. Prefer exact reserve over push/resize initially to avoid the separate recommend policy/arithmetic and extra append/resize loops. Prove a fresh source-backed public caller that constructs one initialized element, saves a data alias, reserves larger capacity, checks size/value preservation and the new owning representation, then destroys the object. Add a non-growing reserve caller that preserves the saved alias, plus an ordinary stale-read negative across growing reserve. Ownership and allocation separation must be derived from the actual constructor/local-receiver source and current model dependencies; never assume the desired invariant at the caller entry.

Preserve named v2 overlap, disjoint-tail same-allocation and borrowed-buffer contract-gap negatives without reclassifying them as C++ executions or solver refutations. Qualified v3 domain-refusal controls must include allocation-identity overlap even when byte-disjoint, nonfreeable backing, forged model/version/source pins, omitted copy/store/free/frame stage and silently inserted desired ownership assumptions. Only then review fresh per-model bodies/helper dependencies and the caller's exact method/model/canonical/lowering bindings, ordinary goal ownership/closure, RTE/init/smoke, lifecycle and task hypotheses under normal public read/write/schema/budget rules.

A qualified candidate with distinct IDs/source/version/method admission keeps original std_models.py/v2, accepted CEA records and pending count actual source untouched. It must remain explicit that candidate creation is not default-catalog promotion, generic caller adoption or full C++ equivalence. A later global version promotion needs the complete affected constructor/copy/move/swap/growth/dtor/caller closure and normal dependency invalidation; an isolated grow law cannot supply it.
