## Constructor checklist

- **C1 — Entry/receiver writes:** start from arbitrary well-formed H and a writable receiver region. The receiver may be uninitialized. Map the three actual null/zero field stores in order; prove each store's validity and exact field initialization. Do not read old fields or assume an existing vector invariant.
- **C2 — Zero branch:** n==0 performs no allocation or element store; establish null data, zero size/capacity, initialized receiver and empty initialized/value ranges. This is the source's null-empty branch, not a proof of every nonnull zero-size allocation case.
- **C3 — Allocation call:** n>0 and n<=m establish allocator domain, n*sizeof(T)<=SIZE_MAX and representable count. Under the existing success assumption, create a fresh unbounded identity, exact logical extent/type, live allocation-base ownership and initially uninitialized cells. Preserve all prior live allocations and receiver storage; allocation must not overlap them.
- **C4 — Capacity store:** prove writable receiver, capacity=n, size still zero, and persistent correct allocation identity/extent. Treat value as the by-value entry scalar; overwriting receiver storage must not change it through an alias.
- **C5 — Prefix establishment:** F(A,0,value)=A gives empty initialized/equal-value prefix; k=0 satisfies 0<=k<=n. Do not assume initialized or filled contents for the entire new range.
- **C6 — Prefix step:** under k<n, prove pointer formation, typed writable access and k within allocated extent; evaluate the original RHS; perform exactly one store of value at k, setting that cell's initialization bit. Derive initializedness and equality for prefix k+1 and frame every other location. In particular, prove the j<k and j==k cases rather than importing a completed-fill theorem.
- **C7 — Loop numeric/termination checks:** k increment cannot wrap because k<n<=m; n-k is nonnegative and decreases. Preserve n, value, receiver data/size/capacity and allocation identity across the loop. The source loop frame permits only k and fresh data[0..n-1].
- **C8 — Loop exit/final size:** combine k=n with proved prefix facts; execute size=n, then derive every conjunct of vector_valid, original size and quantified value postconditions. Initialization of spare capacity must not be fabricated; this constructor happens to allocate exactly size==capacity==n.
- **C9 — Whole frame/allocation clause:** for every pre-existing observable location outside *this, preserve its value/initialization and allocation state. Fresh-region writes are justified by genuine allocation, not by a blanket receiver-only heap havoc. Existing aliases to *this observe its permitted field changes; aliases to other pre-existing objects remain unchanged.

An initialized receiver may already contain an old data pointer. The original constructor overwrites that pointer without freeing its target: the bridge must preserve that old allocation, even if now unreachable from this. “Receiver is new/empty,” “incoming heap is empty,” or “no escaped aliases exist” would strengthen the original contract.

## Destructor checklist

- **D1 — Precondition expansion/read:** map initialized receiver fields, size/capacity constraints and live buffer range. Read the actual data value before releasing it; capture old capacity/data in prestate terms for the original postcondition.
- **D2 — Null branch/free precondition:** capacity==0 implies data==null; release(null) preserves heap. Positive capacity plus the original freeable clause proves a live allocation-base release, not an interior pointer release. Preserve identity through void-pointer conversion.
- **D3 — Release event:** kill the selected allocation identity exactly once. Invalidate access through every escaped pointer carrying that identity, including interior pointers and aliases outside the vector; freeing only the capacity window is insufficient if the incoming allocation is larger. Do not replace aliases with null or merely clear a receiver field.
- **D4 — Original post:** from the actual kill event derive positive-old-capacity ==> !valid(old(data)). The predicate must use poststate liveness and prestate pointer identity, not numerical address comparison or assumed desired postcondition.
- **D5 — Frame:** assigns nothing means no memory-content store, while frees permits the selected allocation's lifetime transition. Preserve values and initialization of unrelated live identities and all other allocation state. Do not translate assigns nothing to complete heap equality, or preserve permissions for released cells.
- **D6 — Aliases/receiver overlap:** the written precondition does not explicitly separate receiver and data. Do not silently assume the receiver remains live or its fields can be read after free. If a typed-layout relation excludes some overlaps, prove that implication from the original semantics; do not insert a caller separation premise. Any permitted overlap in the arbitrary incoming heap must be handled by releasing the entire selected identity, potentially including receiver storage. The original old-expression post does not itself require receiver liveness afterward.
- **D7 — Subsequent operations:** stale read/write, interior/base double release and reconstructed vector-invariant use must fail safety unless independently justified. New allocation may reuse an address but must not revive old identity permissions; unrelated identities stay live. Null free remains a no-op. This requires one shared heap interpretation for generic caller reads and imported contracts.

## Expected positives and negative controls

Plausible original int positives: zero-count field/post/frame goals; positive-count prefix induction; receiver/element initializedness; arithmetic/index/variant checks; fresh-allocation frame; correct-base release; destructor invalidation and unrelated-allocation frame. They are not yet proved.

Expected failures: omitted/wrong-index store breaks value/init or access goals; wrong allocation extent breaks access; wrong released identity breaks original invalidation or frame; stale alias read and second release fail safety; assuming initial receiver fields initialized is unjustified. False candidate claims include “the destructor resets fields,” “the destructor preserves vector_valid,” “all initializedness survives free as access permission,” and “aliases become null.” Constructor's final initialized prefix or destructor invalidity may not appear as unproved callee contracts/axioms.
